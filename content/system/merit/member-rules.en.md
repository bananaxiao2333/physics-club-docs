## Foreword · Why a merit system was needed

The day-to-day running of the Physics Club depends on dues, activity funding, experiment materials, shared equipment and member contributions.
Where the past relied on hand-kept accounts alone, problems readily arose such as **scattered records, incomplete vouchers, difficulty for members to check anything, and activity costs that were hard to trace**.
The merit system was established in order to record the club's funds and the changes in members' accounts in a **uniform, transparent and traceable** way.

> The goal of the merit system is not to make members take on complicated financial work, but to let every member know clearly:
> how their own account increases, how it is used, how it is deducted when they take part in an activity, how a reward is credited,
> how it is handled when they leave the club, and how they can look things up and appeal when a dispute arises.

**In one sentence**: merit is the **account record unit** used internally by the Physics Club,
for recording matters such as members' payments, activity costs, collection of materials, reward payments, project settlement and the handling of withdrawal.

## I · What the merit system is

### 1.1 The nature of the system

The merit system is the Physics Club's internal **dues and activity record system**. It runs around the master account, the fund pools,
the individual accounts and the public ledger, and serves to raise the transparency and auditability of how the club's funds are used.

> Merit is used only under the club's internal rules. It is **not open to people outside the club, is not used as a payment instrument outside the club,
> and offers no free transfer between members, no buying and selling, no discounting to cash and no other form of external circulation**.

### 1.2 The key points members need to understand

| Item | Public explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **Account records** | Every member has an individual account, for viewing their own merit balance and their records of crediting, use, rewards and withdrawal |
| **Master account** | The master account records changes in the Physics Club's funds as a whole, and distinguishes the fixed fund pool from the circulating fund pool |
| **Public ledger** | Members can view the club's public ledger and the details relevant to themselves; sensitive information is redacted or shown by tier according to the rules |
| **Reward payments** | Rewards are recorded by the system and paid after review by administrators; **no fixed payment or fixed return is promised** |
| **Refunds and withdrawal** | Refunds are in principle combined with account closure or the handling of withdrawal; **no partial withdrawal at any time is provided for** |

## II · Basic concepts

The definitions below are primarily those of **member-side use**, and do not cover internal back-office operating parameters.

| Concept | Member-side explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **Merit** | The account record unit used internally by the Physics Club, for recording payments, spending, activities, rewards, refunds and project settlement |
| **Individual account** | The merit record account held in each member's name, showing that member's balance and the flows concerning them |
| **Master account** | The master record account for changes in the Physics Club's funds and merit as a whole |
| **Fixed fund pool** | The public management part of the master account, used to support the club's public affairs, advance payments, shared equipment, basic guarantees for activities and other matters confirmed by the rules |
| **Circulating fund pool** | The day-to-day use part of the master account, more directly tied to members' activities, collection of materials, project settlement and use of individual accounts |
| **Public ledger** | The club's overall accounts and project records shown to members, including the necessary income, expenditure, balance, vouchers and explanations |
| **Reward payments** | Merit records paid by the club into members' accounts on the basis of activity performance, completion of tasks, project contributions or other published rules |
| **Project settlement** | After an activity, materials pack, takeaway order, food, club merchandise or other project is completed, the recording of income, cost, deductions and balance |

## III · Accounts and fund pools

### 3.1 Master account and individual accounts

The merit system takes the **master account** as its core. The master account records changes in the Physics Club's funds as a whole,
and individual accounts record each member's own changes in merit.
**Every change in an individual account should ultimately have a corresponding record in the master account.**

Every member can view:

- Their own account balance
- Their own crediting details
- Their own use details
- Their own reward details
- Their own withdrawal-handling record

The management accounts are responsible for registering, reviewing and maintaining the system's general ledger.

### 3.2 Fixed fund pool and circulating fund pool

The master account has a fixed fund pool and a circulating fund pool beneath it.
The fixed fund pool mainly carries the club's public affairs and management needs;
the circulating fund pool mainly carries the needs of members' day-to-day activities, collection of materials and project settlement.

> Once a member's payment or other crediting enters the system, it goes into the corresponding fund pool and account according to the **public notice or activity description in force at the time**.
> The specific ratio, scope of application and arrangements for change are governed by the Physics Club's formal announcements, activity descriptions or system records.

### 3.3 Simplified ledger relations

To make this easier for members to understand, the system's simplified relations can be written as:

```text title="Simplified ledger relations"
L = Σ Aᵢ
```

- `L` —— the total balance in the circulating fund pool that relates to individual accounts
- A~i~ —— the individual account balance of the i-th member

When a payment or crediting of amount `x` enters the system and, under the rules in force, a use ratio `r` is counted into the fixed fund pool:

```text title="Crediting into the fixed fund pool"
Fixed fund pool increase   = x × r
Individual account increase = x × (1 − r)
```

!!! note "The ratio is not decided ad hoc by an individual"
    The specific ratio is governed by the **public notice, payment description, activity description or system record in force at the time**.
    **Completed historical records are in principle not changed back at will.**

## IV · Earning merit

### 4.1 Earning by payment

When a member pays their dues, or tops up in accordance with an activity's rules, the system records the corresponding merit according to the amount actually credited and the rules in force.
A payment record should contain **the amount, the time, the handler, the review status and the necessary voucher**.

> Part of a payment may be allocated to the fixed fund pool under the rules in force, for the club's public affairs;
> the remainder enters the member's individual account. **Members should read the corresponding explanation before paying.**

### 4.2 Earning by reward

When a member takes part in an activity, completes a task, organises a project, maintains equipment, assists with management, submits a piece of work or makes another contribution,
they may receive a reward under the activity's rules. **A reward payment should have a stated reason and a review record.**

### 4.3 Earning by correction

If a system record contains an error, the account may be corrected after verification by means of a **correcting record**.
A correcting record should state its reason, and **the original record should not simply be deleted**.

### 4.4 Other ways of earning confirmed by public notice

The club may provide other ways of earning merit according to its activity arrangements or project needs,
but should explain them **before the activity begins or before the crediting**, and keep a record of them in the system.

## V · Using merit

### 5.1 Where it can be used

| Scenario | Usual explanation |
| --- | --- |
| **Activity costs** | Experiment activities, materials for talks, competition entry fees or the cost of organising an activity |
| **Experiment materials** | Consumable packs, electronic components, tool accessories, small pieces of apparatus and so on |
| **Food and collective purchasing** | Food, drinks or other shared supplies ordered together during an activity |
| **Club merchandise** | Badges, stickers, notebooks, commemorative items and so on |
| **Other matters** | Governed by the activity description, system announcement or rules confirmed by the management team |

### 5.2 How use is processed

1. The member reads the activity or project description and confirms the merit required, the content of the activity and how settlement works;
2. After the member signs up, places an order or confirms participation, the system generates a **pending record**;
3. After the activity is completed or the materials are collected, the system forms a **formal use record**;
4. If there is an overcharge, an undercharge, a cancelled activity or a recording error, an administrator deals with it through a **correcting record**.

### 5.3 Restrictions on use

!!! warning "The boundary members must know"
    Merit **does not support** free transfer between members, **does not support**
    private buying and selling, **does not support** use in transactions outside the club, and **does not support** partial withdrawal of cash at any time.

    Any conduct that bypasses the system, trades privately, or exploits a loophole to obtain merit
    may lead to **restriction of the account or handling by the club**.

## VI · Activities, materials and project settlement

Activities, materials and collective purchasing are the most common situations in which the merit system is used.
So that members can clearly understand the basis of any deduction, **each project should in principle form a project record**.

| Project type | Key points of settlement |
| --- | --- |
| Activity project | Income is formed from the number of sign-ups and the budget; actual costs are written off after the activity ends, and the balance goes into the fixed fund pool |
| Collection of materials | Paid from the individual account, external costs written off, the difference goes into the fixed fund pool |
| Food and collective purchasing | Recorded according to what was actually ordered and how it was shared |
| Club merchandise | Recorded according to production cost and the quantity collected |

Once settlement is complete, members can see the deductions concerning them in their individual account,
and the club's overall project record appears in the public ledger.

## VII · Reward payments

The aim of the reward mechanism is to **make members who take on tasks actively, organise activities, shoulder the club's work and contribute a great deal visible**.

The system **does not pay out at will**; it is controlled through activity rules, the setting of the reward pool and multi-signature confirmation:

1. **Cap**: in non-wartime conditions, the cap on the reward pool is for the time being limited to **less than the profit of the corresponding activity**;
2. **Customisation**: the amount in the reward pool is customised **for each activity**;
3. **Multi-signature**: after the reward pool is opened each time, **more than 3 administrators must complete the grant multi-signature** before it can be paid out.

Once the reward payment is complete, the system records **a decrease in the reward pool** and **an increase in the individual account**.
For an ordinary member, what this looks like is a reward arriving in their account;
for the officers, what it is in essence is the release of an already-locked reward amount in the fixed fund pool to an individual account.

!!! note "A reward is not a promise of return"
    In “the key points members need to understand”, the white paper states explicitly:
    a reward “**does not promise a fixed payment or a fixed return**”.
    This is consistent with the positioning of contribution credits in [the 04.27 assembly, page 9](../../collection/gatherings/0427.md).

## VIII · The public ledger and access to information

The public ledger is one of the core values of the merit system.
The early material states explicitly: **secret bookkeeping readily leads to a crisis of trust**.
The system therefore has to let members view the record of the club's overall fund operations and the records relating to their own account.

The goal of the public ledger **is not to publish every personal privacy**, but to publish the information necessary and related to the club's public affairs:

- The size of the fund pools
- The purpose of public expenditure
- Activity settlement
- Payments from the reward pool
- Major account adjustments

### Permissions

| Role | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary member | Mainly **read-only** |
| Administrator | Entry, review and initiating operations |
| Important matters | Require **confirmation by several administrators** |

If a member objects to a record, a deduction, a reward or a refund, they may raise an **appeal or apply for a review**.

---

## IX · Refunds, withdrawal and account closure

!!! warning "Refunds are bound to withdrawal"
    A refund is **in principle combined with account closure or the handling of withdrawal**, and **no partial withdrawal at any time is provided for**.

    If a member wants a refund, they must go through an application and review process;
    after the real funds flow out, the corresponding record is **written off**, and the account is closed or frozen.

---

## X · Members' rights

| Right | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Right to be informed** | To understand matters concerning their own interests, including account balance, crediting, use, rewards and withdrawal-handling records |
| **To consult the public ledger** | To view the club's public ledger and the details concerning themselves (sensitive information is redacted or shown by tier according to the rules) |
| **Right of appeal** | To raise an objection, add an explanation or apply for a review when they object to a record, a deduction, a reward or a refund |
| **To require the basis of disclosure** | To require, where necessary, that the rules on which a decision rests be made public |

---

## XI · Members' obligations and restrictions on use

- Use merit according to the club's rules, and **do not put it into circulation outside the club in any form**;
- Do not bypass the system to trade privately;
- Do not exploit loopholes to obtain merit;
- Read the corresponding explanation before paying;
- When a recording error is found, deal with it through the **correction process** rather than asserting a claim oneself.

Breach of the obligations above may lead to restriction of the account or handling by the club.

## XII · Frequently asked questions

??? question "Can merit be transferred to someone else?"
    No. Merit **does not support free transfer between members, nor private buying and selling**.

??? question "Can merit be exchanged for cash?"
    No. Merit **does not support discounting to cash, nor partial withdrawal at any time**.
    A refund is in principle bound to **account closure or withdrawal from the club**.

??? question "Are rewards fixed?"
    No. Rewards are paid according to the activity rules and the setting of the reward pool,
    and **no fixed payment or fixed return is promised**.

??? question "How do I know where the money went?"
    Through the **public ledger**. Members can view the size of the fund pools, the purpose of public expenditure,
    activity settlement, payments from the reward pool and major account adjustments, as well as the details concerning themselves.

??? question "What if a record is wrong?"
    It is dealt with through a **correcting record**. A correcting record should state its reason,
    and **the original record should not simply be deleted**.

??? question "What if I am not satisfied with the outcome?"
    You may raise an **objection, add an explanation or apply for a review**.
    The system explicitly requires that one-sided clauses such as “the club holds the final right of interpretation” be avoided.

## Appendix · Member workflow

1. **Pay dues** → read the notice in force → the system allocates by ratio → the individual account is credited
2. **Check** → sign in to the individual account → view the balance and the flows
3. **Take part** → read the activity description → sign up or place an order → a pending record is generated → it becomes a formal record on completion
4. **Collect materials** → pay from the individual account → external costs written off
5. **Receive a reward** → the reward pool is opened by multi-signature → the individual account increases
6. **Check and appeal** → consult the public ledger → apply for a review if there is an objection
