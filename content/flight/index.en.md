## In one sentence

This was not “an origami activity”. It put physics, engineering, competition and creativity on the same field:
**registration, test flights, laser ranging, live projection, the awards, the record** — all of it carried through by a student club on its own.

> Paper for sails, a flight path over Yingdong.

## The three moments in the original plan

The run of show document split the event into three parts: **fold → measure → challenge**:

1. **Fold · starting from a single crease**
   The plan set up folding instruction and free flight, so that participants first worked with their hands and then observed how each test flight differed.
2. **Measure · turning the flight path into data**
   The notes from the 4.27 assembly defined the result as the straight-line distance between the start point and where the paper plane landed, and divided the site into a preparation area and a flight area.
3. **Challenge · improving through one attempt after another**
   The competition format set out a preliminary flight, the official flight, a final for the top eight, and the student–teacher challenge.

## Site layout

The site map from the 4.27 assembly marks the **preparation area**, the **flight area** and the **start point**;
the run of show document instead set up a **free flight area**, a **distance challenge area** and a **creative display area**, run zone by zone.

The boundary was marked out with isolation posts and warning tape (5 cm × 46 m);
the entrance, the start baseline, the test-flight area and the direction of the toilets were each indicated by one of **7 guide signs**.

## Teaching: 6 folding patterns

The teaching corner was staffed by club members throughout (the run of show names **胡益成、肖子扬** as teaching specialists),
teaching **6 high-performance paper plane folding patterns** hand over hand and handing out the diagrams on site.

> From basic structure to flight optimisation, so that everyone can pick it up quickly.

## What we really wanted to see

Of course the competition was about who flew the furthest. But what the spoken script wanted to see was more than that:

> Whose design has the most ideas, who is best at solving problems with physics thinking,
> who can take a simple paper plane and really give it a bit of a technological feel.

Because where the launch velocity comes from, how energy is converted during the flight,
how the wing shape and the position of the centre of gravity together decide how it performs —
these questions are physics in themselves.

## About the results

!!! warning "This site publishes no results"
    The surviving material records the **preparation** only. The three on-site forms (registration sheet, sign-in sheet, results sheet) are **all blank templates**,
    with no entries at all. This site therefore does not fill in placings, distance results or a “school record” figure.
