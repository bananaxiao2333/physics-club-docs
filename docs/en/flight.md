# The Flight Project

<p class="memorial-kicker">THE MEMORY COLLECTION · THE FLIGHT PROJECT</p>

!!! warning "This is a planning archive, not a record of results"
    The surviving materials document **preparation**, not outcome.

    All three event forms — registration, sign-in, and results — survive as
    **blank templates**. There is no record of any distance, placing, or
    "school record". **This site therefore publishes no results.**

## What the event was meant to be

Not a "folding activity". It put physics, engineering, competition and
creativity onto a single field: **registration, test flights, laser ranging,
live projection, awards, and a record attempt** — all run by a student club.

> With paper as the sail, the journey reaches as far as the east.

## The three movements

The runbook splits the event into **fold → measure → challenge**:

1. **Fold — starting from a crease.** Folding lessons and free flight, so
   participants handle the material before observing how each flight differs.
2. **Measure — turning a trajectory into data.** The 27 April assembly defined
   the score as the straight-line distance from start to landing, with the
   field divided into a preparation area and a flight area.
3. **Challenge — improving through repetition.** The format provided test
   flights, official flights, an eight-finalist round, and a staff–student
   challenge.

## Competition format

**Version A** — source: the event runbook

- **Group stage**: 1 test flight + **3** official flights; best score advances,
  selecting **8 finalists**
- **Final**: **5** flights, combined with the group-stage score; decides gold,
  silver, bronze, and the **school record holder**
- **Staff–student challenge**: groups arrange their own matches against
  teachers, who may use club equipment; **prizes awarded win or lose**

**Version B** — source: the division-of-labour document

- **Group stage**: 1 test flight + **2** official flights
- **Final**: **3** flights

The two versions disagree on flight counts and are both preserved.

## How the event made itself auditable

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Chief judge (*sink*) | Rule interpretation, validity of flights, final dispute rulings |
| Check-in | Competitor check-in, keeping the flight area clear |
| Ranging | Laser rangefinder (500 m range) recording flight data |
| Scoring | Live projection of scores, for transparency |
| Recording | Scores logged on a whiteboard, later published on a board |

The choice of laser ranging over a tape measure follows directly from the
"straight-line distance" rule: the measurement has to be reproducible and
reviewable in the event of a dispute.
